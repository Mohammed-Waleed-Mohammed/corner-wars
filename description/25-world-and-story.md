# 25 — The World & Story Bible

The canon for **The Fall of the Citadel**. Every name, date and rule of the setting lives here. Missions (file 28), factions and commanders (file 27), biomes (file 26) and the in-game Codex (`src/content/lore.ts`) must agree with this file. **When the story changes, change it here first.**

---

## A. Premise

> In 2047 something older than humanity broke through the salt floor of the Saltglass Basin: a black-glass spire three kilometres high, humming with an energy no instrument could name. For sixteen years it powered the world, and a new order rose to guard it: **the Concord**. In 2063 the Concord tried to open the spire's **Heart**. The Citadel broke. Twelve great shards fell across the continents, took root, and grew into lesser Citadels. Each one is a fortune in gold and a key to the Concord's orbital weapons. The old order fell with it.
>
> Eight years later four powers fight over the shards. And the shards have begun to sing to one another.

**Logline:** a war of custody, profit, freedom and faith over the pieces of a god-machine. Nobody understands it, and everyone wants it.

## B. Tone & themes

- **Grounded military sci-fi with one impossible thing in it.** Soldiers, tanks, mud and radio chatter, built around an object nobody understands. Think C&C Generals' grit plus a slow cosmic mystery. Never camp or comedic. Dry humour is fine.
- **No pure villains.** Each faction is right about something:
  - **the Concord** about danger;
  - **Halcyon** about rebuilding;
  - **the Cantons** about freedom;
  - **the Choir** about the Citadel being more than a machine.

  Each is also wrong about something, and it costs lives.
- **Custody vs. ownership.** The core question: can anyone be trusted to hold power that can end the world? The campaign answers "not alone, and not forever".
- **The Citadel is never fully explained.** It is characterised by what it *does*, never by what it *is*. Any answer the story gives must open a bigger question.

## C. The Citadel — the rules of the impossible thing

Every gameplay mechanic has an in-world reason. Writers must not contradict these.

| Mechanic (file) | In-world explanation |
|---|---|
| The Citadel can't be destroyed (07) | Nothing humans have built can mark its surface. The 2048 "Salt Wars" proved it with a tactical warhead. |
| Capture by presence: 12 s uncontested, within 3 tiles (07) | The **Uplink** at a Citadel's base accepts command codes only from whoever physically surrounds it, unchallenged. Nobody knows why. The Concord called it "the Custody Rule". |
| +2 gold/s while held; rich gold near the centre (03, 07) | A Citadel's **Resonance** field draws gold-bearing ore up through the crust. Veins are richest nearest a spire, which made "Citadel gold" the currency of the post-Fall world. |
| +20% production while held (07) | Resonance speeds up fabrication: machine tools run cooler, alloys set faster and assembly drones never tire. |
| **Command Energy** (07) | Resonance charge banked in the Uplink. Holders spend it to call on the Concord's old infrastructure, which still obeys any Uplink. |
| Artillery Strike | The **Hammer**: Concord orbital mass-driver slugs. |
| Reinforcements | **Waypost** drop pods from automated Concord supply caches. |
| Battle Frenzy | A Resonance surge through soldiers' neural combat rigs. |
| Repair Surge | A **nanite bloom**: Concord medical and engineering nanites, woken by the charge. |
| Ion Strike | The **Lances**: the Concord's orbital particle-beam satellites, built "to defend the Citadel". |
| Each map has one Citadel at its centre | Each map is the site of one of the **twelve Fallen** (great shards). Each regrew into a lesser Citadel within days of landing. |
| Biomes differ (26) | The shards fell everywhere: desert, tundra, delta, volcanic arc. Near the oldest shards the land itself turns to crystal (the Scar). |
| Commander abilities (27) | Each commander brings their own assets (Concord shields, Halcyon logistics, Canton know-how, Choir rites). These are not Citadel powers, so they recharge on their own. |

**Resonance.** This is the Citadel's energy. It is purple-white to the eye, felt as a low hum in the teeth and unmeasurable by any instrument except its own Uplinks. Dr Amara Osei's 2048 **Osei Coupling** made it possible to draw power from it. Prolonged exposure causes **"the Hearing"**: vivid dreams, then a constant sense of a voice just below sound. The Choir calls this grace.

**The Heart.** A chamber at the core of the original Citadel, found by deep scan in 2061. Its Resonance output is millions of times the surface field. Opening it caused the Fall.

**The Chorus (2070–).** Since 2070 the Fallen have pulsed in unison, on a 41-hour cycle, the same length as the Fall itself. The pulses are getting stronger. Nobody agrees on what they mean.

## E. The world after the Fall — regions

The old borders don't matter any more. People name lands for what the Fall made of them. Each region is a biome (file 26).

- **The Verdant Reach:** temperate river valleys and old farmland, the most "normal" place left. Canton heartland. Shards: *Ashby*, *Greywater*.
- **Saltglass:** the great salt desert around Ground Zero and the shard-strewn wastes beyond it. Salt crust fused into glass by the Fall's heat. Halcyon's richest mines. Shards: *Kessel*, *Meridian Flat*.
- **The Frostmarch:** sub-arctic tundra, snowfields and frozen lakes. Concord Remnant fortresses guard two shards here. Shards: *Hold Varga*, *Coldwater*.
- **The Cinder Coast:** a volcanic arc woken by two shard impacts, with black ash plains and lava rivers. Halcyon's Prism Labs and the Choir's pilgrim roads. Shards: *Pyre*, *Obsidian Gate*.
- **The Mirelands:** a vast southern delta of marsh, mangrove and flooded towns. Canton river-folk. Shards: *Lantern*, *Deepfen*.
- **The Scar:** the land around Ground Zero, and around the oldest shards, slowly turning to violet crystal. Resonance is strongest here, and so is the Hearing. Choir territory. Shards: *Halo*, *Thornspire*. **Ground Zero** sits here too: the Heart's crater, not a Fallen.

That makes twelve Fallen named on the maps, plus Ground Zero.

## G. Characters

### Commanders (playable — mechanics in file 27)

**Marshal Ilsa Varga — Concord Remnant.** Born 2013. A Custodian captain at the Basin on the night of the Fall, she carried the Concord flag out of the burning city with 300 survivors. She is stern, precise and incorruptible, and haunted by the order she obeyed that night (she held the perimeter while the Protocol ran). Sees custody as penance.
*"We held it once. We will hold it again — and this time we will not open the door."*

**Captain Yusuf Haddad — Concord Remnant.** Born 2040. He was a cadet in the Hammer batteries during the Fall. Now an artillery officer and Varga's protégé, he is brilliant and idealistic, and believes in what the Concord was meant to be more than in what it was. He quietly questions whether sealing the shards is right.
*"Give me coordinates and ninety seconds. The sky will do the rest."*

**Director Celeste Marchetti — Halcyon Dynamics.** Born 2019. She is Halcyon's Chief Operating Director: impeccable, pragmatic and never cruel when cruelty is unprofitable. She genuinely believes rebuilding the Grid will save more lives than any army. She thinks in quarters and in centuries.
*"Everything has a price, Marshal. I'm simply the only one who reads the invoice."*

**Dr Imani Achterberg — Halcyon Dynamics.** Born 2027. She heads Halcyon's R&D skunkworks, **Project Prism**, which builds weapons that "tune" Resonance. She is brilliant and impatient, and indifferent to ethics until they become data. She was Osei's last doctoral student.
*"Fear is just a hypothesis nobody has tested."*

**Captain Dara Okafor — Free Cantons.** Born 2042. She grew up on river barges during the Dimming, became a smuggler of medicine, and is now the elected war-captain of the Cantons. Quick, warm and funny, she is fierce about her people, and suspicious of anyone who says "for the greater good".
*"We don't need your Citadel. We need what it does for us — light, heat, gold. We'll keep it, thanks."*

**Ezra "Old Wolf" Lindqvist — Free Cantons.** Born 2004. A retired Concord combat engineer who left the Corps in disgust in 2060 and joined the Cantons. He is the master of their roads, bridges and ambushes, grumpy and laconic. He was Aurelian Kade's sapper in the Salt Wars, and he knows how Kade fights.
*"Every road is a trap if you build it right."*

**The Cantor (Ysolde Rhee) — Choir of the Hollow Sun.** Born 2022. A Concord xenolinguist, she was inside the Citadel when the Heart opened. She walked out of Ground Zero three years later, unharmed, hearing the **Song**. She is serene, gentle and absolutely certain. She believes the Reconvergence is a birth, not a weapon.
*"Listen. It is singing your name. It always was."*

**The Penitent — Choir of the Hollow Sun.** A masked war-leader who never raises his voice above a whisper. He commands the **Ashen Host**, the Choir's armies, with cold Concord-trained precision. **Secret (revealed in mission 11):** he is **First Director Aurelian Kade**, who ordered the Ascension Protocol. He survived inside the Heart and wants to open it again, *properly* this time, and become the voice the Citadel answers to.
*"I opened the door once. I know what is on the other side. You don't."*

### Supporting cast (speakers in missions and the Codex)

- **Dr Amara Osei** (born 1997). She discovered the Osei Coupling and is now Varga's science adviser: frail, sharp, and the story's conscience. She designs the **Custody Protocol**, a way to quiet the Chorus without opening the Heart.
- **MERIDIAN.** Halcyon's corporate command intelligence. It is polite and precise, and speaks in risk percentages.
- **Juniper.** A Canton signals operator, 17, who taught herself to read Resonance pulses on scavenged radio gear. She is the first to decode that the Chorus is a *countdown*.
- **Deacon Vex.** A Choir field commander, the Penitent's voice on the battlefield. He is fervent and cruel, and the recurring antagonist of Acts I–II.
- **Sergeant Tomás Ruiz.** Varga's veteran NCO, at her side since the Fall. Steady, dry and loyal.
- **First Director Aurelian Kade** (born 2001). He led the Concord from 2056 and ordered the Ascension Protocol. Charismatic and visionary, he was certain the Heart would end scarcity for ever. He was listed as missing after the Fall.

## H. Campaign arc (summary — missions in file 28)

- **Prologue, 2063: "Custody".** Captain Varga holds the Basin perimeter on the night of the Fall.
- **Act I: Embers (Concord Remnant, 2071).** The Remnant tries to secure the Frostmarch and Saltglass shards. It finds the Choir excavating Ground Zero, and finds that the Penitent wears Kade's signet.
- **Act II: Currents (Free Cantons).** The Cantons fight Halcyon "acquisitions" and Concord "custody". Lindqvist recognises Kade's tactics. Juniper decodes the Chorus: the Fallen are counting down to a second Opening.
- **Act III: Chorus (Halcyon, then the Choir, then the finale).**
  - Halcyon tries to price and then weaponise the Chorus (Prism).
  - Playing the Cantor, we learn the Penitent's real aim, and the Cantor begins to doubt.
  - **Finale, "Reconvergence".** Varga takes Ground Zero and holds the Heart while Osei runs the Custody Protocol. The Penitent is unmasked. Kade chooses to stay inside as the Heart seals. The Chorus goes silent, *for now*.

## I. Canon rules & open mysteries

- **Never explain** what the Citadel is, who built it, or whether it is alive. Characters may hold theories. The narration takes no side.
- The Song and the Hearing are real experiences, but the story never confirms that a mind is behind them.
- Kade's fate after the finale is **unresolved** (sequel hook). So is whether the Custody Protocol is permanent.
- There are exactly **twelve Fallen**. Ground Zero is not a shard; it is the wound.
- Nobody has ever destroyed or moved a Citadel. Nobody ever will.
- The Lances and the Hammer obey any Uplink. That is why every faction wants a Citadel: the shards are the keys to the sky.

## J. Writing style guide

- **Briefings:** second person, present tense, under 120 words, ending on a concrete goal. Date and place first.
- **Radio dialogue:** at most 2 lines per speaker turn, 160 characters per line, clipped military cadence. A name tag before each line.
- **Vocabulary:**
  - **Fallen / shard:** a lesser Citadel.
  - **Uplink:** the capture point.
  - **Resonance / the Hum:** the energy.
  - **the Hearing:** exposure symptoms.
  - **the Chorus:** synchronised pulses.
  - **Ground Zero / the Heart:** the original site.
  - **Citadel gold:** the economy.
  - **Custody:** holding a shard.
- **Don't:** use real-world nations, religions or politicians. Don't write gore. Don't joke about the Fall's dead.
